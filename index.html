Graham Platner: The Record

On June 9, 2026, Graham Platner won the Maine Democratic U.S. Senate primary with 156,084 votes — more than any Democratic Senate candidate in the state's history. Thirty-one days later he withdrew, denying every allegation against him. This page documents the whole case: his own words, every allegation and what its claimed corroboration actually is, the money spent against him, and every major negative story — so you can weigh the record yourself.

How to read this page — the standpoint, declared
1

The presumption rule. Platner denies the allegations. Until an item of corroborating evidence is verified by an investigation independent of the outlet that published it, this page treats the item as unverified and takes his denial as the operative account. Every item below carries a visible verification box. As of the last update, every box is unchecked.

2

What this page does not claim. Documenting the money and the timing does not prove — and this page never states — that any accuser is lying. Allegations are recorded as allegations, denials as denials. No charge, court finding, or adjudication exists anywhere in this record, in any direction.

3

Why the political context is here. $11,077,173 in FEC-filed independent expenditures opposed Platner's campaign; $323,426 supported it — roughly 34 to 1. That is part of the documented record of this race, and no honest reading of the media record can ignore it. The page presents the record and lets you weigh it.

His claims, checked

"The things that have been claimed did not happen."
the open question — the presumption rule applies  The claimed events are this record's open question, and Rule 1 above governs it: he denies them, and until an item of claimed corroboration is verified by an investigation independent of the outlet that published it, this page treats the denial as the operative account. The verification ledger tracks exactly that — and as of the last update, every verification box on it is unchecked.
"Much like October when the first attack started…"
consistent with the record  The first negative wave began Oct 16, 2025 — CNN and Politico published deleted-Reddit-post stories the same day; 12 major stories followed that month. See the catalog.
"…the news that was created the week before the primary…"
consistent with the record  The WSJ and NYT explicit-texts stories ran the same day, May 30 — ten days before the primary; the NYT dating-history article ran June 4 — five days before.
"I only have until July 13th until I am officially the nominee."
date correct characterization imprecise  July 13 was the statutory deadline (21-A M.R.S. §374-A): the last day his withdrawal would let the party replace him on the ballot. The assault allegation published July 6; he was out by July 10.
"…at a strength of over 150,000, the largest number in the history of Maine primaries…"
150,000+ verified record is real but narrower  Official final: 156,084 votes, 72.13%. The verified record: most primary votes of any Democratic U.S. Senate candidate in Maine history since 1918. The broader all-offices, all-parties version is unverified. See the mandate.
"Bigger organizations, the national level party, the bigger donor networks, they have all committed to spending no money in this race if I'm in it."
partially documented  Reported, not FEC-filed: Senate Majority PAC (the main Democratic Senate super PAC) reserved and then pulled Maine ad spending around July 6, reportedly wanting Platner off the ballot. Documented in press reporting; his broader characterization is his own.
